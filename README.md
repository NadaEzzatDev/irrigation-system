# 🌱 Mini Irrigation System | Interactive Wiring Experience

### From confusing wires to confident connections!

**What if students could think like engineers before connecting a single wire?**

The **Mini Irrigation System – Interactive Wiring Experience** transforms a traditional Arduino wiring lesson into a guided, hands-on digital journey. Instead of presenting students with a complete circuit and a list of connections to memorize, it invites them to explore, predict, answer, build, and experiment.

Every connection has a purpose. Every question encourages thinking. Every step brings the system closer to life.

## ✨ Explore the Experience

- **🔍 Discover Before You Build** — Explore the electronic components and understand their roles before assembling the circuit.
- **🧠 Think, Predict & Answer** — Solve interactive questions throughout the journey and receive immediate feedback.
- **⚡ Watch the Circuit Come Together** — Reveal the wiring step by step as you progress through the experience.
- **💧 Experiment with Soil Moisture** — Adjust the moisture level and observe how the irrigation system responds to changing conditions.
- **✅ Build It in Real Life** — Follow a practical wiring checklist to recreate the circuit using physical components.

## 🛠️ More Than Just Connecting Wires

This experience introduces the engineering concepts behind the project through practical examples and interactive challenges:

- Why an Arduino cannot power a water pump directly.
- How an **L298N H-Bridge motor driver** controls the pump.
- How an external battery supplies power to the motor driver.
- How a breadboard distributes power and connections.
- Why all circuit components need a common ground (**GND**).
- How analog sensor readings help the Arduino make decisions.
- How the system follows the logic: **Detect → Decide → Water**.

## 🧩 Components

- Arduino UNO
- Soil Moisture Sensor
- Mini Water Pump
- L298N H-Bridge Motor Driver
- 9V Battery
- Breadboard
- Jumper Wires

## 🚀 Start Exploring

Open the interactive experience, navigate using **Next / Back** or the keyboard arrow keys, and challenge yourself to understand each connection before moving forward.

**The goal isn't just to make the circuit work. It's to understand why it works.**
